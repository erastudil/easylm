# A table, a rate, and a group

You can already count, share, and name a percent. This book is how those moves behave when the numbers came from the world, not from a made-up pile of apples.

---

## 1. One row is one thing

Imagine a grid. Each row is one thing: a person, a day, a town, a trial. Each column is one question asked of every row: age, rainfall, yes or no. That grid is a **table**.

If a cell is empty, that question was not answered for that row. Empty is not zero. Zero is an answer.

**Check.** Three rows of "cups of coffee today": 2, 0, blank. The blank does not mean the person drank none. It means you do not know.

---

## 2. A typical value

A column of numbers wants a single name for "about how much."

Line the numbers up from smallest to largest. The middle one is the **median**. Half the rows sit at or below it, half at or above. A single giant value does not drag it far.

Add the numbers and divide by how many rows you added. That is the **mean**, the everyday "average." A single giant value drags it.

For "how spread," a cheap start is the smallest and the largest. Later packs will name variance. You already have enough to distrust a mean without a spread.

**Check.** 2, 3, 3, 4, 100. Median 3. Mean 22.4. If someone says "the typical value is 22," they used the mean and hid the 100.

---

## 3. Per what, in what time

12 crashes in a year in a town of 4,000 people is not the same fact as 12 crashes in a year in a city of 400,000.

Write per. 12 crashes per 4,000 people is 3 crashes per 1,000 people. The second town, at the same mix, would have 1,200 crashes. If it had 12, it is safer per person, even though the raw 12 matches.

A comparison with units on both sides is a **rate**.

Percents are rates per hundred. They still need the whole. "Up 50%" of a small pile can be a smaller change than "up 5%" of a large pile.

**Check.** Always name: count, whole, time. "12" is not a claim.

---

## 4. A sample is not the pile

You cannot ask every fish in the lake. You catch some. Those caught are a **sample**. The lake is the **population**.

If you catch only at the dock, you have dock fish. A number from a sample is a claim about the sample first. It becomes a claim about the lake only if the catch was mixed the way the lake is mixed.

Random here means: every fish had a chance. It does not mean sloppy. A sloppy catch is a different bias.

**Check.** A poll of people who already opened this app is a sample of people who opened this app. It is not a sample of a country.

---

## 5. After, not because

Ice cream sales rise. Drowning deaths rise. Both follow hot months. Heat is a third thing. Ice cream did not cause the drownings.

A **confounder** is that third thing. Two columns can move together because both follow a third column.

To get closer to because, change one thing on purpose and keep the rest still, or compare groups that were assigned without picking the outcome. Methods will name trials and causal graphs. This pack's rule: "after" is not "because," and a pretty line is not a mechanism.

**Check.** Ask: what else moved? what was assigned? what stayed still?

---

## 6. A count that is also a story

"3% of users failed." Failed how. Of how many. In what window. Compared to what.

A small sample can swing. 1 fail in 10 is 10%. 1 fail in 1,000 is 0.1%. The same one person.

Methods will name p-values and confidence intervals. You do not need those names to demand a denominator, a time, and a comparison.

Official counts of people, money, weather, and disease live on government doors: census, statistics agencies, weather services, health agencies. Fetch them. A chart on a poster is not the door.

---

## 7. What you can do today

1. Put the claim in a table with a denominator.
2. Name the typical value two ways if the pile has a giant.
3. Write the rate with units.
4. Say who was in the sample.
5. Name one other thing that could have moved.

When a later pack shows a p-value, a poll, an inflation rate, or a medical percent, it is using this chapter. If the denominator is missing, the chapter is not done.
