# Lunyu as a walk

You can already read English. This book walks a Chinese book of short spoken lines. The object is one saying you can hold in a day, then the name the tradition uses for that saying and for the book that keeps it.

A survey of living schools sits in the philosophy pack. A survey of communities of worship sits in the religion pack. Another old Chinese primary text, the Dao De Jing, sits in the tao_te_ching pack. This book names those doors and stops. It owns the walk through the Lunyu.

---

## 1. A saying

Lin sits at a kitchen table after a class. The teacher did not assign a chapter of theory. The teacher said three short questions, paused, and sat down. Lin writes the three questions on a card that will fit in a pocket. Tomorrow Lin will try to live one of them for an afternoon. If the card is still in the pocket at dusk and none of the three questions has met a real hour, Lin was busy and the card did no work.

That pocket card is the object of this book. A **saying** here is a short spoken line, kept in a collection, that you can fail or keep in a day. It is not a biography. It is not a school of thought. It is not a temple. It is one line you can hold up against an afternoon.

People later bound many of those lines. The bound book is the **Lunyu**. English often calls it the Analects. The Chinese Text Project Analects door in `LINK_INDEX.md` also lists "The Analects of Confucius" and "The Confucian Analects". The same door shows twenty books, from Xue Er to Yao Yue. It dates the work to the Spring and Autumn through Warring States span **480 BC-350 BC**. It prints James Legge's English and names the source as *The Chinese Classics*, volume 1, 1861. The digital Chinese base text on that door is the Wuyingdian *Shisan jing zhushu* printing of the *Lunyu zhushu*.

A second official door, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Confucius, first published 31 March 2020 and substantively revised 2 May 2024, writes the name Confucius as a Latinized mix of the surname Kong with the honorific Master (*fuzi*). It gives the traditional life span **551-479 BCE**. It calls the book the *Analects* (*Lunyu*). Those two clocks are not the same clock. One is a traditional life. The other is a window the text-project host gives for the work. Do not average 551 and 480. Keep both, and keep the host that owns each.

The first line of the first book, Xue Er, on the Chinese Text Project Xue Er door, in Legge's 1861 English, is three questions:

> The Master said, "Is it not pleasant to learn with a constant perseverance and application? Is it not delightful to have friends coming from distant quarters? Is he not a man of complete virtue, who feels no discomposure though men may take no note of him?"

Three questions. Learning. Friends who come from far. A person who does not sour when no one notices. Lin's card is already a small Lunyu: a line, a try, a dusk check.

**Check.** Copy those three questions onto a card. Number them 1, 2, and 3. Cover the page. Write what object each question names in one short phrase: practice of learning; a friend who arrived; a person who stays even when unnoticed. If you wrote "wisdom" three times, you named a fog. Put the afternoon back in.

New case: a neighbor leaves one sentence on a fridge: "Hang the spare key on the hook by 18:00." That is a saying you can fail. Count the chairs: 1 key, 1 hook, 1 time. If you write a paragraph about thoughtfulness and the key is still in your pocket at 18:10, the fridge line did not get walked.

## 2. Learning

Lin reads the first question once, nods, and closes the book. Nodding is not the first question. The first question is about learning *and* applying it at times, not about a single pass of the eyes.

The Xue Er door keeps that as the Master's opening move: it is pleasant to learn with constant perseverance and application. Later in the same book the Master describes a person who does not hunt a full stomach or a soft room, who is earnest in work and careful in speech, and who goes to people of principle to be set straight. Legge's line ends: "such a person may be said indeed to love to learn." Learning here is a habit that leaves a mark on food, rooms, work, and speech. It is not a certificate.

The same book gives a daily tool. The philosopher Zeng says he examines himself on three points each day: whether, in transacting business for others, he may have been not faithful; whether, in intercourse with friends, he may have been not sincere; whether he may have not mastered and practiced the instructions of his teacher. Three questions, every day. The teacher's line is not finished when it is heard. It is finished when it has been practiced, or when the miss is named.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door quotes a disciple's picture of roots: "The gentleman works at the roots. Once the roots are established, the way comes to life" (1.2). Learning here is work at a root, not a tour of branches. Later readers call this first book **Xue Er**. You do not need the Chinese graph on day one. You need the move: read, return, try, name the miss.

Zi Xia, still on Xue Er, raises the bar in the other direction. If a person turns from a love of surface beauty to a love of the virtuous, serves parents with full strength, serves a prince with a life, and keeps sincere words with friends, then even if people say that person has not learned, Zi Xia will say that person has. The card can be blank and the life can still be the study. The page can be full and the life can still be unread.

**Check.** For 7 mornings, pick one instruction you already have (hang the key, return a pan, read one saying aloud). Mark a tally each morning you actually did it. On morning 8, write the tally out of 7. If the tally is 1 and you "feel dedicated," the feeling is not the tally. The object is the 7.

New case: you copy Xue Er 1.1 onto a card on Monday and do not look at it again until Sunday. Write how many practice days you had: 0 after the copy, unless a day in between met a question. Copying is not perseverance. It is a start.

## 3. Humaneness as a first look

Four people sit down to eat. There are 3 bowls of rice. Bo is nearest the bowls. Bo can take 2 and leave 1 for the other three, or Bo can pass first and take last. No law clerk is in the room. The extra bowl in Bo's hand would still be an extra bowl.

The Lunyu keeps a word for the habit of not making a world that only fits you. Later English often says **humaneness** or benevolence. The Stanford Encyclopedia door writes *ren*, and says the *Analects* sometimes defines it as "caring for others" (12.22), and that Confucius tells Yan Yuan it is "overcoming oneself and returning to ritual propriety" (12.1). Those later books are not on the Xue Er page. Treat that English as the encyclopedia's report. Open the Yan Yuan door in `LINK_INDEX.md` for Chinese and Legge side by side. Until you open it, do not paste a private wording as if it were the 1861 line.

Xue Er already plants a root. The philosopher You says people who are filial and fraternal are rarely fond of offending superiors, and that no one who dislikes offending superiors has been fond of stirring up confusion. Then: "Filial piety and fraternal submission! - are they not the root of all benevolent actions?" Legge's "benevolent actions" is the same family as *ren*. The first look is not a slogan about loving the world. It is a near debt: parents, older and younger, the people in reach.

The Master, still on Xue Er, cuts a fake: "Fine words and an insinuating appearance are seldom associated with true virtue." A soft voice can sit next to a hard grab. The rice test does not ask how warm Bo sounded.

A youth, in the same book, should be filial at home, respectful to elders abroad, earnest and truthful, overflowing in love to all, and close to the good. After those acts, if strength remains, then polite studies. The studies come after the near debt, not instead of it.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door also reports a reflexive line later readers pair with the biblical golden rule: "Do not impose upon others those things that you yourself do not desire" (12.2, with 5.12 and 15.24). That door warns that reading the line as a free-floating axiom is a later habit, and that some scholars treat those passages as interpolations. This book does not settle that fight. It asks you to try the move on 3 bowls.

**Check.** 4 people, 3 bowls. If Bo takes 2, write who is short: 3 people sharing 1 bowl. Write whether Bo would accept that split if the hands were reversed. If your sentence is "Bo should be nice," you named a fog. Put the bowls in the sentence.

New case: 5 coats on a peg and 6 people leaving in rain. You take 1 coat that is not yours because you "need it more." Write the imposed thing: 1 person walks out with no coat who had hung one. If you would not want your own coat taken by that story, the 12.2 move already failed, even before you open book 12.

## 4. Ritual as a form

Mei stands at a door. A guest has arrived. Mei can grunt and walk back to the stove. Mei can also take the guest's wet coat, point to a chair, and wait until the guest sits before talking business. The second path is a form. It uses extra seconds: take the coat, hang it, wait for the sit. The wet coat is still a coat. The form is those seconds.

The Lunyu keeps a word for those shared shapes. English often says **ritual**, rites, or propriety. The Chinese word the Stanford Encyclopedia door writes is *li*. On Xue Er, the philosopher You says that in practicing the rules of propriety, a natural ease is to be prized, and that the old kings' way is beautiful in this. Then the brake: if you prize ease and show ease *without* regulating it by the rules of propriety, that likewise is not to be done. Ease without the form collapses into whatever the strongest person in the room already wanted. Form without ease is a wooden greeting that no one can breathe in.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door reports Confucius condemning ritual without reverence (*jing*) at 3.26, and ritual or music that notice only offerings or instruments at 17.11. It reports a disciple who wanted to drop a sheep from a seasonal sacrifice, and the reply: "though you care about the sheep, I care about the ritual" (3.17). Open Ba Yi on the Chinese Text Project for Legge on 3.17 and 3.26. The form is not the object on the altar. It is the shared act, done in a real inner state.

You Ruo on Xue Er also ties funeral care to the character of a people: careful funeral rites for parents, and sacrifice when they are long gone, and "the virtue of the people will resume its proper excellence." A town that cannot bury its dead with attention will not magically be gentle in the market.

Zi Gong, still on Xue Er, praises the poor person who does not flatter and the rich person who is not proud. The Master says those will do, but they are not equal to the poor person who is cheerful and the rich person who loves the rules of propriety. Love of the form is a higher test than merely not being a snob. The rich person who loves *li* is not collecting etiquette for show. They are letting a public shape limit what their money could otherwise grab.

**Check.** Write 8 steps for Mei's door: see the guest; stand; take the coat; hang it; point to the chair; wait for the sit; ask if they want water; then talk business. Now skip steps 3 and 4 (the coat stays in the guest's arms). Write what broke: 2 skipped steps, 1 wet coat still in someone's hands. If you say "Mei was still polite," you counted a mood. The form counted the coat.

New case: a table of 6. Custom is that the last plate is offered around once before anyone takes seconds. You take seconds on the last plate without the offer. Write the missing round: 5 people not asked. The ease of your hunger ran without the form.

## 5. A person as a check

A saying that never meets a person is a poster. The Lunyu keeps testing lines against a kind of person you could point to in a room.

On Xue Er, Legge's English calls that person "a man of complete virtue" and, in other lines, "the superior man" or "the scholar." The Stanford Encyclopedia door writes **gentleman** for *junzi*: a moral ideal distilled from old exemplary behavior, open to anyone who will work at the roots. This book uses **a person you could check**. Legge's "superior man" is an 1861 handle. It does not mean a person born above you. It means a person whose pattern you could watch for a week.

Watch the pattern on Xue Er. If the scholar is not grave, there is no veneration and the learning will not be solid. Hold faithfulness and sincerity first. Have no friends not equal to yourself. When you have faults, do not fear to abandon them. Food does not have to fill; the room does not have to be easy; work is earnest; speech is careful; company is chosen so that the person can be rectified. That list is a checklist, not a halo.

The third question of 1.1 is the quiet test: no discomposure though people take no note. The last line of Xue Er turns the same camera around: "I will not be afflicted at men's not knowing me; I will be afflicted that I do not know men." A person who needs the room to clap has not yet met the first book's person. A person who cannot tell who is in the room has failed a different, harder check.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door lists five central behaviors: benevolence (*ren*), righteousness (*yi*), ritual propriety (*li*), wisdom (*zhi*), and trustworthiness (*xin*). Those five are a later teaching set, not a score out of 100. This chapter keeps one object: a person whose week would show the saying, or show the miss.

**Check.** Pick 1 saying from Xue Er 1.1. Watch yourself for 3 workdays. Each day, write a mark: kept, missed, or no chance. After 3 days you should have 3 marks. If you have a feeling of improvement and 0 marks, you checked a mood. The person is the 3 days.

New case: a stallholder is praised in a speech on Friday and short-changes 2 customers on Saturday, 1 coin each. Write the person-check: 2 short coins, 1 speech. The speech is not the person. The Saturday is.

## 6. Government as example

Ken runs 12 stalls on one street. Ken arrives 40 minutes late for 4 mornings. By the fifth morning, 7 of the 12 stalls open late. No new rule was posted. The street copied a body.

The Lunyu treats rule as that kind of copy, not as a stack of penalties first. On Xue Er the Master says, of a country of a thousand chariots: there must be reverent attention to business, and sincerity; economy in expenditure, and love for men; and the employment of the people at the proper seasons. Legge's "thousand chariots" is a size class for a state that can field that many war carts. The number is a scale, not a modern census. The moves are four: attend, be true, spend less, love people, and do not pull farmers off the field in the season when the field needs them. Pulling 1,000 people from planting to paint a palace is a government that ate its own season.

Zi Qin asks why the Master, arriving in a state, always comes to know its government. Does he ask, or is it given? Zi Gong says the Master is benign, upright, courteous, temperate, and complaisant, and thus he gets the information. The asking is already a government lesson: a person who arrives gentle and exact learns a court that a barging person would only scare.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door, in its political stretch, compares the gentleman's influence on the people to wind on grass, forcing it to bend (12.19). It reports the claim that if the people do not find the ruler trustworthy, the state will not stand (12.7). Open Yan Yuan on the Chinese Text Project when you want those Legge lines on the primary host. The move is already in Ken's street: the late body is the wind.

Employment of the people at the proper seasons is a number you can fail without a palace. If Ken calls all 12 stallholders to a meeting at 07:00 on market morning, Ken stole the hour the street sells. If Ken calls them at 16:00, when stalls are slow, the same meeting costs less grain.

A survey of offices, votes, and clerks sits in civic books, not here. This chapter only walks the Lunyu's claim: the person in front is already the law people copy.

**Check.** 12 stalls. Ken late 40 minutes for 4 mornings. Write the copied lateness if 7 stalls each lose 40 minutes on morning 5: 7 times 40 is 280 stall-minutes. If you write "morale fell," you named a fog. Put 280 on the paper.

New case: Ken posts a fine of 5 coins for a late stall and then arrives late, unpaid. Write two rows: the posted 5, and Ken's 0. The street now has a rule that does not apply to the body that wrote it. The wind still blows. The grass watches the body, not the sign.

## 7. Friendship

Ada has one friend, Jo, who walks 9 streets to sit at Ada's table. Jo could have stayed home. The walk is the gift. If Ada spends the evening glancing at a second conversation on a small screen, Jo's 9 streets bought a half-person.

Xue Er opens friendship in the second of the three questions: it is delightful to have friends coming from distant quarters. Distance is part of the object. A friend who spent a day on the road is not the same fact as a friend who lives upstairs.

Zeng's daily three includes friends as the middle point: whether, in intercourse with friends, he may have been not sincere. Faithfulness at work, sincerity with friends, practice of the teaching. Friendship is a check, not a mood that excuses the other two.

The Master, still on Xue Er, says to have no friends not equal to yourself. Legge's line is stern. It does not mean "collect only famous people." In the same stretch he says hold faithfulness and sincerity first, and when you have faults, do not fear to abandon them. A friend equal to you, in this book, is a person whose pattern can correct yours. A flatterer is not equal. A person you use as a mirror that always smiles is not equal. You Ruo adds: when the people you lean on are proper to be intimate with, you can make them guides.

Zi Xia's learned person is the one whose words with friends are sincere. Zi Gong's pair, poor without flattery and rich without pride, is a friendship test as much as a class test: can you sit with Jo without selling yourself or sitting above.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door notes that trustworthiness (*xin*) may be rooted in friendship between people of the same status (1.4, 5.26) and is also the quality that lets a person be given responsibilities. Friendship in the Lunyu is practice for public trust, not a private club that cancels public trust.

**Check.** Jo walked 9 streets. Ada is present for 20 minutes of a 50-minute visit, then on a screen. Write the unpaid walk: 9 streets bought 20 minutes. The remaining 30 minutes were a room with a body and no friend. If you write "they are close," you named a fog.

New case: 5 evenings in a row Jo asks Ada to listen to a real trouble. Ada talks about Ada for 4 of the 5. Write 1 evening paid, 4 unpaid. Equality in this book would mean Ada can also be corrected, not that Ada owns the air.

## 8. Words that match

Sora tells a stall she will stand it from 14:00 to 20:00, 6 hours, so the owner can take a child across town. Sora arrives at 14:00, leaves at 18:00, and says "I was there." The words and the hours do not match. The child was booked against 6.

Xue Er worries this split from the first pages. Fine words and an insinuating face are seldom true virtue. You Ruo says that when agreements are made according to what is right, what is spoken can be made good; when respect is shown according to what is proper, one keeps far from shame. A word you cannot make good was not yet an agreement in this book's sense. It was a sound.

Zi Xia will call you learned if, among other acts, your words with friends are sincere. The Master wants a person careful in speech and earnest in doing. Speech is cheap until a later hour can fail it.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door reports a theme across the *Analects*: judging people by deeds rather than words, citing 1.3, 2.10, and 5.10. It also reports, in its political stretch, a later teaching often named the rectification of names: offices, titles, and words should match the work they claim, or speech and government both rot. The detailed dialogue sits in book 13, Zi Lu, which the Xue Er page does not print. Open the Zi Lu door in `LINK_INDEX.md` for the primary Chinese and Legge. Until you open it, keep the move you can already fail: a title, a promise, or a praise that does not match the hours.

A country of a thousand chariots needed sincerity next to reverent attention. Sincerity here is not warmth. It is the match between what was said and what the street can count.

**Check.** Promised 6 hours, stood 4. Write the unpaid 2. If Sora says "I showed up, so the words were true," Sora counted arrival. The stall counted 6. Arrival is not 6.

New case: a sign on Ken's street says "open 08:00." 3 stalls open at 08:00, 9 open at 08:25. Write the match: 3 true signs, 9 false, 25 minutes late. If Ken's speech still says the street opens at 08:00, the speech is now one of the 9.

## 9. Two voices in the text

Open Xue Er and listen. The Master speaks. The philosopher You speaks. The philosopher Zeng speaks. Zi Xia speaks. Zi Gong speaks. Zi Qin asks. One bound book, several mouths. If you flatten every line into "Confucius said," you lose the object in front of you.

The Stanford Encyclopedia door makes that flattening a scholarly problem, not a mood. It says the philosophy of Confucius is historically underdetermined: early sources keep biographical details, but dialogues in texts like the *Analects* reflect several representations. A conventional view, strong for about a thousand years, treats the twenty transmitted chapters as the original teachings. That view draws in part on Ban Gu (39-92 CE), who described the *Analects* as compiled by first- and second-generation disciples. Zhu Weizheng's 1986 argument is that missing attributed quotations and title references before the second century BCE mean the *Analects* should not be treated as the oldest stratum by default. Michael J. Hunter finds acute interest in the *Analects* only in the late second and first centuries BCE.

The same door lists archaeology that a stranger can put on a timeline. A 1973 excavation at Dingzhou in Hebei dates to 55 BCE. A 1990s find at Jongbaekdong in Pyongyang dates to between 62 and 45 BCE. The 2011-2015 excavation of the tomb of the Marquis of Haihun in Jiangxi dates to 59 BCE and is thought to contain two lost chapters of a 22-chapter Qi version, with titles the door gives as "Understanding the Way" and "Questions about Jade." Transmitted *Analects*: 20 chapters. Han report of a Qi version: 22. Do not average 20 and 22. They are two reports of two objects.

Sima Qian (c. 145-c. 86 BCE), on that door, identifies 77 direct disciples and some 3,000 students in a broader training count. Those are the historian's numbers, written centuries after the traditional death date. They are not a classroom roll you can re-count this afternoon.

Two voices are not only two speakers. They are two pulls that the same bound book will not pre-settle for you. The Stanford Encyclopedia door reports a story of Upright Gong, who testified that his father stole a sheep, and the judgment that a father would conceal such a thing for a son, and a son for a father, and that uprightness is found in that concealment (13.18). The same encyclopedia reports, as a government line, that if the people do not find the ruler trustworthy, the state will not stand (12.7). Family concealment and public trust are both in the tradition's Confucius file. A later chapter of this library does not get to erase one with the other. You name both, then you pick in a case, and you say which line you used.

Zhu Xi (1130-1200), on the same door, read the *Analects* inside a Four Books curriculum that spread in China, Korea, and Japan. That is a later voice laid on the early voices. The Chinese Text Project also keeps a *Lunyu jizhu* door and a *Lunyu zhushu* door so you can see commentary as commentary, not as the first line.

**Check.** On Xue Er, count speakers with a named saying: the Master, You, Zeng, Zi Xia, and the Zi Qin-Zi Gong exchange. That is 5 voices if you count the exchange as one scene, or 6 if you count both disciples. Write your count and your rule. If you wrote "Confucius" for all of them, you erased You, Zeng, and Zi Xia.

New case: a parent takes a sack of flour that is not theirs. A child knows. A market clerk asks. Write two sentences, not one blend: the 13.18 pull (conceal inside the family) and the 12.7 pull (a street that cannot trust words will not stand). Then pick, and write which unpaid chair you accepted: the clerk's missing sack, or the child's testimony against a parent. If you average them into "be balanced," you did not walk either voice.

## 10. A check against a primary door

This book has been walking one object: a saying you can hold, then the name. The last check is not a new idea. It is a door.

Open the Chinese Text Project Analects door in `LINK_INDEX.md`. Copy four facts the page itself prints: the work name *Lunyu* / The Analects; the count of books in the table of contents (the fetch used here showed **20**, Xue Er through Yao Yue); the date line **480 BC-350 BC**; the translation credit **James Legge** and the source year **1861**. If the live page now shows a different date line or a different book count, the live page wins. Do not keep this book's copy out of loyalty.

Open Xue Er on the same host. Copy the Chinese of the first saying and Legge's three questions. Cover this textbook. Recite the three objects: learning with practice; a friend from far; a person who does not sour when unnoticed. If you cannot, you have been touring a famous book. You have not held a saying.

Open the Stanford Encyclopedia Confucius door. Copy the traditional life span **551-479 BCE**, the first-publication date **31 March 2020**, and the substantive-revision date **2 May 2024**. Copy one archaeological date from its sources section: Dingzhou **55 BCE**, or Jongbaekdong **62-45 BCE**, or Haihun **59 BCE**. If a date on the live page has moved, keep the door.

The Library of Congress home and the Library of Congress classification-outline door are in `LINK_INDEX.md` so a stranger can look up how a national library classes this shelf. A fetch of those Library of Congress pages returned an interstitial screen, not a catalog record. Treat any class number you did not copy from a live Library of Congress page as **unverified**. Do not borrow a Dewey number from memory and pin it on that host.

If a translation you want is not on a door you have open, teach yourself the move without pasting a private wording. Mark the wording unverified, then fetch Xue Er, Wei Zheng, Ba Yi, Li Ren, Yan Yuan, or Zi Lu on the Chinese Text Project and read Legge there.

What this book does not own: a survey of living philosophy; a survey of religion; the Dao De Jing as a primary walk. Name those packs and stop.

**Check.** Paper with six copied items: 20 books; 480 BC-350 BC; 1861; 551-479 BCE; 31 March 2020; one tomb date (55, or 62-45, or 59). If any of the six is missing, you described the Lunyu and you did not meet it.

New case: a friend quotes a saying "from Confucius" that you cannot find in Xue Er. Do not finish the quote from memory. Open the Analects search on the Chinese Text Project door. If the line is not there, write unverified. If it is there, write the book name the host prints (Xue Er, Wei Zheng, or another) and the Legge sentence the host prints. The friend is not the door.
