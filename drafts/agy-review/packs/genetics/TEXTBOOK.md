# A trait that is copied

You can already count. This book is the next act: a trait that is copied.

The living biology pack at `stacks/biology/` is the survey of cells, organs, and living things. This pack names that door and stops. The walks here are inheritance and copy walks.

MedlinePlus Genetics is the consumer door for how a copied trait meets health. That landing page keeps pages on more than 1,300 health conditions with a genetic basis, pages on more than 1,400 genes, and a walk of each of the 23 pairs of human chromosomes plus mitochondrial DNA. NCBI is the National Center for Biotechnology Information. It keeps gene records, genome records, and sequence search, and it advances science and health by providing access to biomedical and genomic information. OpenStax *Biology 2e* is the long school walk. Authors, ISBN, and edition dates on that details page are unverified here. NIH is the nation's medical research agency, part of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. A load-bearing genome count, a gene total, a string length: fetch MedlinePlus or NCBI, or write unverified.

This book is public general education.

## 1. A trait that runs in a family

Mira sets 11 plates on a Sunday table. Four of the people who sit down have a dent in the left thumb, the same dent her grandfather had. The dent is not a story they tell. It is a mark you can see.

Count the table. Mira, her sister, her father, her father's brother: those four show the dent. Seven people at the table do not. The mark skipped Mira's mother and both of Mira's children so far. A mark can sit in a family and still skip a seat.

The object of this chapter is that mark as it travels. Ordinary English: a trait that runs in a family. People name the travel **inheritance**. The name is a handle for the walk you already counted.

A trait can be a dent, a white lock of hair, the way a seed coat looks, or a condition that several cousins share. The family is the first lab. You write who showed it, who did not, and who has not been born yet. You do not yet write why.

Share on this table: 4 of 11 show the dent. 4/11 is the count of this room. It is not a law for every room with this family name. Next Sunday, two cousins stay home. The table is then 9 people, and the 4 who show the dent may still be 4, or 3, or 2, depending on who stayed home. The trait did not change. The pile you counted did.

MedlinePlus asks, on a page you can open, what it means if a disorder seems to run in a family. That same genetics door keeps pages on more than 1,300 health conditions with a genetic basis. A named condition, a count of how often it appears, a claim that a family always shows it: fetch that door, or write unverified. Do not finish a frequency from a memory of a poster.

A trait that runs in a family is not proof that a unit was copied. Shared food, shared rooms, and copied units can all pile into the same table. This book will separate those piles. Today you only write the table.

**Check.** A new table: 7 people, 2 with a white forelock. The two are a grandmother and one grandchild. The parent in between does not show the lock. Write three lines: who showed it, who did not, who sits on the path between them. If you only counted the 2, you dropped the parent on the path. A second new case: 9 cousins, 0 with the dent. The dent can still run in a wider family you have not sat with. The 0 is a count of this room, not a law for every cousin. A third: 13 people, 5 with the dent. Share 5/13. If you wrote 4/11 from Mira's table onto this one, you copied the wrong room.

## 2. A unit that is copied

A baker keeps one wooden stamp. Each loaf gets a press from that stamp. The loaf is not the stamp. The press is a copy of the cut in the wood.

Each child of Mira's father received a copy of some unit that can make the dent. The unit is small. The body is large. The unit is what is copied. The body is where the trait may show.

People name that unit a **gene**. MedlinePlus keeps a page titled "What is a gene?" and a catalog of more than 1,400 genes. That 1,400 is the count of gene pages on that door, not a count of every gene in a human body. The total number of human genes is unverified here. Fetch NCBI Gene or MedlinePlus, or write unverified.

One unit, copied, can sit in many bodies. Five children of one mother each received a copy of one of her units. That is 5 copies in 5 children, plus the copies still in the mother. The mother's two copies are the next chapter. Today: one unit is copied into the next body.

A unit can be copied and the trait still not show. The unit is the thing that travels. The trait is what you see when the unit, the rest of the body, and the room allow it. Mira's children do not show the dent. They may still hold a copy. You cannot tell from the table alone. You can only write that they do not show it.

The copy is made when a body makes a child. The child is not a blend of two paints poured in a cup. The child received units. Some units from one parent, some from the other. Chapter 3 counts how many of each unit. This chapter only needs the fact of a copy.

OpenStax *Biology 2e* is the long school walk for how a cell holds and passes units. Dates on that details page are unverified here. NCBI Gene is the record door for a named unit. A length, a symbol, a claim about what one unit does: fetch the record, or write unverified.

**Check.** A new mother, 5 children, each child got a copy of the same unit from her. How many of those copies sit in the 5 children? 5. A sixth child is born and gets a copy too. Now 6. If you wrote 1 because it is the same unit, you counted kinds, not copies. This book counts copies. A second new case: 4 children, and you do not know whether the fifth pregnancy received the unit. You have 4 known copies in children, and one unknown. Do not write 5 to make a round pile.

## 3. Two copies

Mira's goat has two copies of the coat-color unit, not one. One copy came from the dam. One copy came from the sire. Most of the units in this book travel that way: two copies in a body, one from each parent.

People name one version of a unit an **allele**. Ordinary English: a copy that may not match the other copy. If the two copies match, the body is **homozygous** for that unit. If they differ, **heterozygous**. Count the two first. Put the names on after you can point at match and mismatch.

When a new kid is made, each parent sends one of its two copies. Which one is a draw. If the dam has copies D and d, half the kids get D and half get d, if the draws are even and you look at many kids. One kid is one draw, not a half kid.

A **loud** copy shows the trait even when the other copy is quiet. A **quiet** copy shows the trait only when both copies are quiet. People name loud **dominant** and quiet **recessive**. The loud/quiet talk is the mechanism. The school names arrive as labels on that mechanism.

Worked case. Dam: D d. Sire: d d. Each kid gets d from the sire. From the dam, D or d. Two equally easy draws, if we say they are equally easy: half the kids D d, half the kids d d. The D d kids show the dark coat if D is loud. The d d kids show the pale coat.

Fourteen kids from that pairing: 8 dark, 6 pale. Half of 14 is 7. The 8 and 6 sit near 7 and 7. That is a count, not a proof that D is loud. A small pile can lean.

Both parents D d. Four equally easy draws: D from dam with D from sire, D from dam with d from sire, d from dam with D from sire, d from dam with d from sire. Write them D D, D d, d D, d d. If D is loud, three of the four draws show dark, one shows pale. Of 20 kids, 5 pale is the even-draw count, because 20 shared into 4 is 5. Of 16 kids, 4 pale. Of 40 kids, 10 pale. Do not treat 5 of 20 as a law for the next 20. The next 20 is a new pile.

Dam D D, sire d d. The dam can only send D. The sire can only send d. Every kid is D d. All 11 kids dark if D is loud. There is no pale draw in this pairing.

MedlinePlus inheritance pages are the door for how a named human condition is said to travel. A claim that a human trait is loud, or that it needs two quiet copies, is a fetch, or unverified. NCBI and MedlinePlus are the hosts for a named gene's copies. Do not finish "this human trait is loud" from a goat walk.

A square of four equally easy draws is the school picture for two parents and one unit. People name that square a **Punnett square**. The square is a picture of the draws. The kids you count are the check.

**Check.** A new pairing: both parents pale, and pale is the quiet trait. Every copy they can send is d. Every kid is d d. All 9 kids pale. If one kid is dark, either a parent was labeled wrong, or a copy changed, or dark is not this unit. A second new case: dam D D, sire d d. Every kid is D d. All 11 kids dark if D is loud. If you wrote half dark, you used the D d with d d walk on the wrong parents. A third: both parents D d, 20 kids, 6 pale. Even-draw was 5 pale. 6 is near 5. Do not "correct" the 6 to 5. The pile is 6.

## 4. A mix

Two units can travel at once without holding hands. Coat color is one unit. Ear length is another. A kid gets one color copy from each parent, and one ear copy from each parent. The color draw does not set the ear draw, in the simple walk this chapter owns.

People name that independence **independent assortment** in the school books. Ordinary English: a mix. OpenStax *Biology 2e* is the long walk. Dates on that details page are unverified here.

Four kinds of kids if each unit has a loud copy and a quiet copy, and both parents are mixed on both units.

Write C or c for color, E or e for ear. Each parent C c E e. A kid's color: 3 of 4 draws show loud color, 1 of 4 shows quiet color, from chapter 3. A kid's ears: 3 of 4 long, 1 of 4 short, if E is loud. If the draws do not hold hands, multiply the shares:

- loud color and long ear: (3/4)×(3/4)=9/16
- loud color and short ear: (3/4)×(1/4)=3/16
- quiet color and long ear: (1/4)×(3/4)=3/16
- quiet color and short ear: (1/4)×(1/4)=1/16

Of 80 kids, share the 80 into 16 equal parts of 5. Then 9 parts, 3 parts, 3 parts, 1 part: 45, 15, 15, 5. Add them: 45+15=60, 60+15=75, 75+5=80. The 80 closes.

Of 32 kids, 32/16=2. Then 18, 6, 6, 2. Add: 18+6=24, 24+6=30, 30+2=32.

Of 96 kids, 96/16=6. Then 54, 18, 18, 6. Add: 54+18=72, 72+18=90, 90+6=96.

Those three piles are even-draw counts. A real 80 can lean. Write the 80 you have. Compare to 45, 15, 15, 5. Do not replace the count with the even-draw.

If the two units do hold hands, the multiply is the wrong walk. Every dark kid might then have long ears, and you will not see a dark short pile. The table you counted is the boss. The mix walk is a claim that the draws did not hold hands.

**Check.** A new mix: 96 kids from two mixed parents, even-draw 54, 18, 18, 6. Put 54+18+18+6 back to 96. A second new case: you count 32 kids and see 18, 6, 6, 2. That matches the even-draw of 32. A third: color and ear do hold hands, so every dark kid has long ears. Then you will not see a dark short pile. The mix walk of this chapter does not apply. Write the table you actually counted. If you invent a 6 in the dark short pile to make 9:3:3:1 look neat, you forged a kid.

## 5. A string

The copy is not a blob in the hand. It is a string of letters that a cell can read and recopy.

Four letters in the usual writing: A, C, G, T. How those four pair, how long the human string is, how many letters sit in one cell: unverified here. Fetch MedlinePlus "What is DNA?" or an NCBI Nucleotide or Genome record, or write unverified.

People name the string **DNA**. A packed piece of that string, with other packing, is a **chromosome**. MedlinePlus keeps a page for each of the 23 pairs of human chromosomes, and a page for mitochondrial DNA, written mtDNA. 23 pairs is 46 chromosomes by ordinary counting, plus the mtDNA that does not sit in those pairs.

Two copies of a unit, from chapter 3, now have a place: one copy on a chromosome from one parent, one copy on the matching chromosome from the other parent. The pair is the 23-pair walk for humans. A different species can have a different pair count. That count is a fetch, or unverified. Do not write 23 pairs onto Mira's goats.

A string 12 letters long, written ACGTACGTACGT. Copy it. The copy should match at all 12 places. Change letter 7 from A to T. Now 11 letters match and 1 does not. The length is still 12. The job of copying is to keep the letters. The job of counting a change is to name the place.

A shorter drill. String of 8: GATCGATC. Copy. Match at 8 of 8. Change letter 1 to C: CATCGATC. Differ at 1 place. Change letters 1 and 8: CATCGATA. Differ at 2 places. Length still 8.

mtDNA is a separate small string. MedlinePlus names it next to the 23 pairs. How many letters it holds is unverified here. Fetch that chromosome door, or write unverified. A trait that travels only through the mother can sit on that string. A claim that a named human trait does so: fetch MedlinePlus, or write unverified.

NCBI Genome and NCBI Nucleotide are the record doors for a named string. BLAST on NCBI is the search that asks whether two strings match. A recorded human string length, a recorded goat string length: fetch, or unverified. The 12-letter drill above is paper. It is not a genome.

**Check.** A new string of 9 letters: GATCGATCG. Copy it. Change letter 3 from T to A. Positions that differ: 1. Length still 9. A second new case: two strings of 8, differ at positions 2 and 8. Two changes. If you wrote 8, you counted the length, not the changes. A third: 23 pairs in a human cell, plus mtDNA. If you wrote 23 chromosomes, you counted pairs as singles. 23+23=46 for the pairs. mtDNA is extra, and its letter count stays unverified on this page.

## 6. A stretch that does a job

One stretch of Mira's string is used as a job list for one task: make this coat-color tool, or do not. The rest of the string is not that job.

People name a stretch that does a job a **gene**, the same word as the unit that is copied. Chapter 2 counted the unit as it travels. This chapter points at the stretch on the string. Same object, two views: the copy that goes to a child, and the stretch that a cell can use.

MedlinePlus "What is a gene?" is the door. Their catalog of more than 1,400 gene pages is a door count, not a body count. NCBI Gene is the record door. A named human gene, a length in letters, a claim about what the stretch does: fetch one of those doors, or write unverified.

A working rule on paper: read the stretch in groups of three letters. Fifteen letters make five groups. Twenty-one letters make seven groups. Eighteen letters make six groups. If a letter is missing, the groups after the gap shift. That shift is a later chapter's change. The three-letter grouping as a measured chemical fact is unverified here. Fetch MedlinePlus or NCBI, or write unverified. The paper count is: length shared into 3.

15 shared into 3 is 5. 21 shared into 3 is 7. 24 shared into 3 is 8. 30 shared into 3 is 10. 14 is not a whole number of groups: 4 groups and 2 letters left over. 16 is 5 groups and 1 letter left. A leftover means the stretch, read this way, does not land on a whole number of groups.

Not every stretch does a job you can name. Some stretches are packing. Some stretches are unknown. An unknown stretch is not a job list you invented. NCBI and MedlinePlus are where a named job is recorded. Empty record: write unverified.

The cell's use of the stretch, and the organs that use the tool, live in the living biology pack. This pack names that door and stops. The walk here is: point at the stretch, count its letters, share them into groups of three on paper.

**Check.** A new stretch of 24 letters. Groups of three: 8. A second: 30 letters: 10 groups. A third: 16 letters: 5 groups and 1 letter left. If you wrote 6 groups for 16, you invented a letter. A fourth: 21 letters, then drop the last 3. Remaining 18 letters, 6 groups. If you still wrote 7, you counted the dropped group.

## 7. A change in the copy

The copy is not always a perfect press. One letter can differ from the source. A stretch of 15 that should read ACGTACGTACGTACG might read ACGTACGTCCGTACG: letter 9 is C instead of A. One change. Length still 15. Groups of three still 5. Group 3 is now TCC instead of TAC.

People name a change in the copy a **variant**, and sometimes a **mutation**. MedlinePlus keeps a page on what a gene variant is and how variants occur. A named human variant, a count of how often it sits in a population, a claim that it causes a condition: fetch MedlinePlus or NCBI, or write unverified.

A change can do nothing visible. A change can break the job of the stretch. A change can make a quiet copy loud, or the other way. The family table from chapter 1 is how you notice. The string from chapter 5 is how you locate. The stretch from chapter 6 is which job might have moved.

If a letter is dropped, the three-letter groups after the drop shift. A 15-letter stretch that loses letter 4 has 14 letters, which is 4 groups and 2 left over, and the groups from that point are not the old groups. That is a different object from a one-letter swap that keeps the length.

Work the drop. Original 12 letters, groups of three: 4 groups. Drop letter 4. Now 11 letters: 3 groups and 2 left. Groups 2 and 3 are not the old groups 2, 3, and 4. One missing letter moved every later group. A swap at letter 4 would have kept 12 letters and 4 groups, with only group 2 rewritten.

Two bodies can differ at many places. Count the places. Do not call every difference a disease. MedlinePlus documents more than 1,300 health conditions with a genetic basis. That is a catalog of conditions, not a count of every change. NIH funds and reports research on how variation meets health. A claim that one letter is the cause of one condition: fetch MedlinePlus or NCBI, or write unverified.

**Check.** Two strings of 11 letters. They differ at positions 4 and 9. Two changes. A new pair of length 20, differ only at position 1. One change. A third: length 15 in one, length 14 in the other, first 3 match, then the shorter one has no letter 4. One drop. If you wrote 15 changes because the lengths differ, you counted length, not places. A fourth: 15-letter stretch, swap letter 9, then share into 3: still 5 groups. If you wrote 4 groups, you treated a swap as a drop.

## 8. Selection as a first look

Copies that leave more copies become more common in the next pile. Copies that leave fewer copies become rarer. That is the first look. The living biology pack owns the wider survey of living things. This chapter owns the count.

A pond holds 100 beetles: 40 green, 60 brown. Birds eat 30 green and 10 brown. Left: 10 green, 50 brown. Total left: 60. The green share was 40/100, which is 2/5. The green share among the living is now 10/60, which is 1/6. Write 2/5 as 12/30 and 1/6 as 5/30. Green got rarer because more of the green copies were eaten.

If the next round of beetles is born from the 60 who remain, and coat color is copied as in chapters 2 and 3, the next pile starts from 10 green and 50 brown parents, not from 40 and 60. The trait ran through a filter.

People name that filter **selection**. Ordinary English: some copies leave more copies. This book does not finish a history of life. It counts one pond.

The filter can be a bird, a frost, a farmer, a drug, a dry year. The arithmetic is the same: write the pile before, write who leaves offspring, write the pile after. If nothing filters, the share does not have to move.

A second pond. 90 plants, 30 dwarf, 60 tall. Frost kills 24 dwarf and 6 tall. Left: 6 dwarf, 54 tall. Total left: 60. Dwarf share was 30/90, which is 1/3. Now 6/60, which is 1/10. 1/3 is 10/30. 1/10 is 3/30. Dwarf got rarer.

A farmer can be the filter on purpose. Rafi keeps long-eared goats and sells short-eared goats before they breed. The next kids are born from long-eared parents. Short ears get rarer in his herd because he was the bird. That is still selection as a first look. It is not a different arithmetic.

**Check.** A new pond: 80 fish, 20 striped, 60 plain. A net takes 16 striped and 12 plain. Remaining: 4 striped, 48 plain. Total remaining 52. Striped share was 20/80, which is 1/4. Now 4/52, which is 1/13. Write 1/4 as 13/52. 13/52 down to 4/52. A second new case: 50 seeds of each of two coat colors, and nothing eats either color. The share does not have to move. Selection is a filter that happened, not a law that a share must change. A third: 100 beetles again, birds eat 20 green and 20 brown. Left: 20 green, 40 brown, total 60. Green was 40/100=2/5. Now 20/60=1/3. 2/5=6/15, 1/3=5/15. The share still moved, because the starting piles were not equal.

## 9. A test

A clinic can read a stretch of the string, or it can look at the trait on the body. Both are tests. Neither one is the family table by itself.

People name a string-reading a **genetic test**. MedlinePlus keeps a Genetic Testing page and a Newborn Screening page. NCBI keeps Gene records and variant records. NIH is the research agency behind much of that work. A named test, what it reads, whom it is for: fetch those doors, or write unverified.

A test that reads one letter at one place does not read the rest of the string. A test that looks at the coat does not tell you which letter sits at position 9. Say what was read. Write the length of the stretch if you have it. Write the place of the change if you have it. If you do not have them, write that you do not.

A reading can fail to match the family table. Eight relatives, none show the loud trait, and a reading says both copies in a child are the loud kind. The table and the reading disagree. Fetch. Do not average a table and a lab. Do not throw away the table because a machine spoke. Do not throw away the reading because the table is older. Open the doors. Find which pile was labeled wrong.

Newborn screening is a test on a new body. MedlinePlus keeps that door. What a given state reads, and on which day, is a fetch, or unverified. A flag on a screen is a reading. A second reading of the same stretch can agree or disagree. Two readings that disagree are two facts. They are not a blended fact.

This chapter owns the idea of a reading. It does not own a clinic visit, a choice, or a treatment. The health pack and MedlinePlus own named conditions. This pack owns how a copied unit is tested as a string or as a trait.

**Check.** A new reading: one letter change at position 7 of a 15-letter stretch. The family table shows 3 of 8 with the trait. Write both facts. Do not replace the 3 of 8 with the letter. A second new case: a newborn screen comes back as a flag, and a second reading of the same stretch does not find a change. You have two readings that disagree. Fetch the lab's door. Do not keep the flag because it arrived first. A third: a coat is pale, and pale is quiet, so both copies should be the quiet kind. A reading of D d disagrees with the coat. Fetch. If you "fix" the coat to dark so the reading wins, you threw away the body.

## 10. A check

A new herd, not used in the earlier chapters. Rafi's goats: one sire, one dam, 11 kids. Dark coat is loud, pale is quiet. Long ear is loud, short is quiet. The dam is dark with long ears, and she had a pale short-eared parent, so she carries quiet copies. The sire is pale with short ears.

Walk each object on this herd.

A trait that runs in a family. Dark coat sits in the dam and in some kids. Pale coat sits in the sire and in some kids. Long ear sits in the dam and in some kids. Short ear sits in the sire and in some kids. Write who showed which mark. The table is 13 bodies if you count sire, dam, and 11 kids.

A unit that is copied. Coat color is one unit. Ear length is one unit. Each kid got copies, not the parent's body. Two units, not one unit with two names.

Two copies. Dam: C c and E e, because a pale short parent gave her c and e, and she shows loud C and loud E. Sire: c c and e e, because he shows both quiet traits. Each kid gets c and e from the sire. From the dam, C or c, and E or e. Half the kids dark if even. Half the kids long-eared if even.

A mix. Color draw and ear draw do not hold hands here. Four kinds, each about 1/4 of 11 if even. 11 does not share into 4 equal whole kids. 11/4 is not a whole kid. Count the 11 you have. Do not force 11 into 2.75.

Suppose the 11 are: 4 dark long, 2 dark short, 3 pale long, 2 pale short. That is 6 dark and 5 pale, 7 long and 4 short. Near half on color. Ears leaned long. A lean in 11 is allowed. Put the four piles back: 4+2+3+2=11.

A string. The coat unit is a stretch on a chromosome. The human 23-pair count is not this goat's count. Goat chromosome number: unverified here. Fetch NCBI Genome, or write unverified. Four letters in the usual writing still apply as a writing. Length of the goat coat stretch: fetch, or unverified.

A stretch that does a job. The coat stretch does the coat job. The ear stretch does the ear job. Lengths in letters: fetch, or unverified. Groups of three on paper wait until a length is fetched.

A change in the copy. If a twelfth kid is dark and the dam was typed c c by a lab, either the type was wrong, or a copy changed, or the sire was not c c. Do not invent the letter. Do not invent a 12th kid to make a pile look even.

Selection as a first look. Rafi keeps the long-eared kids and sells the short-eared kids before they breed. Of the 11, 7 long stay, 4 short leave. The next pile is born from long-eared parents. Short ears get rarer in his herd because he was the filter. The 4 short kids still exist. They do not become long by being sold. They leave this herd's next count.

A test. A lab reads the dam's coat stretch and reports C and c. That reading matches her dark coat plus a pale parent. If the reading had said c c, it would disagree with the dark coat. Fetch. Do not average. A reading of one pale kid should find c c if pale is quiet and this is the unit. If that pale kid reads C c, the reading and the coat disagree.

Put the 11 back one more time. Dark 4+2=6. Pale 3+2=5. Long 4+3=7. Short 2+2=4. 6+5=11. 7+4=11. If any of those sums miss 11, a kid was counted twice or dropped.

**Check.** A last new case, smaller. One unit, dam D d, sire d d, 8 kids: 5 dark, 3 pale. Even-draw half of 8 is 4. The 5 and 3 sit near 4 and 4. A string-reading on one pale kid should find d d if pale is quiet and this is the unit. If that pale kid reads D d, the reading and the coat disagree. Fetch. A second last case: 8 letters copied, one swap at place 5. Length 8, changes 1. If you wrote 5, you named the place as the count of changes.

The objects of this book are the family trait, the copied unit, the two copies, the mix, the string, the stretch that does a job, the change, the filter, and the reading. A case is closed when those agree, and when every load-bearing genome number has a MedlinePlus or NCBI host.
