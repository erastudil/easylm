# Chapter 0. A list the box will follow

You already have a file, a folder, and a box that keeps what you save. Computing is what the box is doing: following a list.

The list is made of tiny steps. Each step looks at a mark, changes a mark, and goes to the next step. A cook's recipe is the same shape, slow, and allowed to be vague. The box is fast and not allowed to be vague. If the list says "add 1 to the number in this slot," it does that, even if you meant a different slot.

Anything a person can write as a finite, unambiguous list of that kind, a box can do, given enough time and shelf. That claim is the content of the Turing machine you will meet in chapter 1. The tape is the shelf. The head is the worker. The states are "which line of the recipe am I on."

If you have never saved a file, open the computers pack first. If a letter standing for a number still fails, open algebra. Then a type, a compiler, and an operating system are names for: what kind of mark, how a human list becomes a box list, and how many lists share one shelf without eating each other.
