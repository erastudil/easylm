# Chapter 0. A promise about money later

You have 100 now. Someone asks to use it and give you 110 in a year. You are selling time. They are buying time.

The extra 10 on 100 is 10%. That percent is the price of waiting, if you trust the promise. If you do not trust it, the extra has to be larger, or you keep the 100.

A **balance sheet** is a table of what is owned and what is owed at one moment. The two sides match because every thing owned was funded by a debt or by the owner's leftover claim. That matching is double-entry. You can learn it as "every pile has a where-it-came-from."

Later chapters discount a stream of promises, price a share, and name a risk. They are still: a number now, a number later, a chance the later number never arrives, and a table that must balance. If percent and a letter for an unknown still fail, open numeracy and algebra. Do not start at CAPM.
