# How to learn from a page

You can already read. This book is the next skill: keeping what a page is doing, checking it, and using it tomorrow.

---

## 1. One object

Open any chapter in this library. The writer is trying to put one working object in your head: a falling stone, a vote, a cell, a promise in a contract.

Before you read for keeps, name that object in a short sentence you could say aloud. "A fraction is a share of a whole." If you cannot say the sentence, you have not found the object yet. Skim until you can. Then read that stretch slowly.

The name the book uses may arrive late on purpose. Let the picture land first. The name is a handle for a thing you already saw.

---

## 2. The question you bring

A page answers a question. If you arrive with none, the page feels like weather.

Write one question at the top of your paper. "What happens to the other side if I do this to this side?" "Who can stop this office?" "How does the body know to bleed less?"

Read to answer that question. When the page answers a different one, note it and return. Curiosity is legal. Wandering until the first question is lost is how a chapter turns into fog.

---

## 3. Paper is part of the book

Eyes can slide. A hand cannot.

When the book shows a step, copy the step onto paper and do it with a new number. When the book describes a part, draw the part and label what you can see. When the book states a rule, write the rule in your own words, then write one case that obeys it and one case that would break it.

If you cannot do the step with a new number, you copied a shape. Go back one paragraph.

---

## 4. A word you do not have

Stop. Do not guess from the rest of the sentence if the word is load-bearing: a number, a name of a part, a verb that changes the claim.

1. See whether this same book taught it earlier. The table of contents is a map, not decoration.
2. See whether another pack in this library is listed as a prior book.
3. Use the pack's link index. Official doors own facts. A random explainer does not.

Put the new word on paper with one example. Then return to the sentence that stopped you.

---

## 5. A number on the page

A number that matters has a home: a standards body, a statute, a measurement institute, a textbook that shows the count.

If the number is a constant, a dose, a legal threshold, or a conversion, open the door in `LINK_INDEX.md` and look. If the door and the book disagree, believe the door and mark the book. If you cannot fetch, write "unverified" next to the number and do not build on it.

Hands in this app can add, convert units, and search the stacks. Use them for arithmetic. Do not trust a remembered constant.

---

## 6. Two kinds of not-knowing

You can fail to know a word. You can fail to know a move.

A missing word is a dictionary problem. A missing move is a practice problem. "I do not know what *hypotenuse* means" is the first. "I cannot see why this side must be the longest" is the second.

For a missing word, find the object. For a missing move, do three small cases on paper. The third case is where the move usually appears.

---

## 7. Teach it back

Close the book. Say the object and the rule to a wall, a person, or a blank page. Use no names you cannot point at.

If you need the book's sentence to say yours, you are still borrowing. Open the book, find the hole, and try again from there. This is the check. It is not a performance.

---

## 8. Tries are the work

The first time through a hard page, you will miss. That miss is the map of what to do next.

Studio in this app marks pass or fail. It does not mark you. Extra tries are how the object settles. A clean first pass often means you already knew the object. The lesson lives in the pass after a fail.

No due date is a feature. A stuck day is a feature. Return. The page does not expire.

---

## 9. A map of a subject

A field is a pile of objects that share a kind of question.

Physics asks what will happen to a measurable thing if we wait. History asks how we know a past event from what remains. Civics asks who may do what to whom under a written rule.

When you enter a new pack, read the first chapter until you can name that kind of question. You do not need the whole syllabus map. You need the question. The rest of the book is cases of it.

---

## 10. When the next book is for someone else

A fluent paragraph can still be wrong. Keep the object, the step on paper, and the door for the number.

Studio marks pass or fail on the work. The record stays on this device. A late night and a slow page are legal.

When a chapter in another pack feels written for a different reader, open the prior pack named in the front of that book, or open chapter 0 if the pack has one. Then come back.
