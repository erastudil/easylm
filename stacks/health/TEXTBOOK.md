# Health Sciences & Human Physiology — Homeostasis, Organ Systems, Immunology & Evidence-Based Medicine

A comprehensive undergraduate textbook exploring human physiology and medical science: the physics and biochemistry of homeostasis, organ system mechanics (cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, neuroendocrine, gastrointestinal), cellular immunology and pathogenesis, pharmacology and pharmacokinetics, emergency first-aid protocols, epidemiological biostatistics, and clinical evidence hierarchies.

---

## 0. Syllabus & Structural Map

Human health is the active, energy-intensive maintenance of physiological homeostasis against entropic breakdown. Claude Bernard termed this the stability of the *milieu intérieur* (internal environment), later formalized by Walter Cannon as homeostasis. To maintain life, tens of trillions of specialized cells cooperate within tight physical and chemical boundaries: arterial pH between 7.35 and 7.45, core temperature near 37°C, and plasma osmolarity near 290 mOsm/L. Health sciences investigate the multi-tiered negative feedback loops that sustain these boundaries and the pathological disruptions that compromise them.

## 1. what health is

**health** (working cut) is the body’s capacity to keep its insides in range and to do the jobs of living — in a person, a family, a place. **disease** is a named disruption of that. **medicine** is the craft that examines, names, and treats, under a license.

four questions, every time:

1. is this **structure**, **process**, or **emergency step**?
2. is a **number** (dose, rate, time, cutoff) required — is it on an official page?
3. is this a **person** (diagnosis, “should I take X,” a wound in front of you)? if yes — clinician / trained first-aider. this book structures the ask.
4. water / food / air / waste — fetch the public-health page before improvising.

**seats.** a licensed clinician (physician, NP, PA, pharmacist, midwife, dentist — name the one) owns diagnosis and prescription. EMS owns the ambulance. poison control owns the ingestion ask. a first-aid certificate owns the trained lay sequence. this textbook owns none of those seats.

**evidence, stacked (do not skip a layer):**

| layer | is | door class |
|---|---|---|
| mechanism | how a part works | biology pack, physiology text |
| observation | a measurement in a person or a sample | lab range, MedlinePlus |
| study | a designed comparison | methods pack; PubMed |
| systematic review | a body of studies | Cochrane / a named review |
| guideline | what a society currently recommends | CDC, WHO, a named specialty society |
| this person | diagnosis and plan | clinician |

a mechanism is not a trial. a trial is not a guideline. a guideline is not this person. do not jump from a pathway diagram to a pill.

**check:** if you cannot say structure vs process vs person, you are not ready to name a drug.

## 2. the body as systems

**anatomy** is structure. **physiology** is function. they pair. human-shaped here because that is the literacy ask; other animals differ — fetch.

**levels:** cell → tissue (epithelial, connective, muscle, nerve) → organ → system → organism. name the level. biology pack owns the cell; this chapter owns the jobs of systems in a person.

systems as **jobs**:

| system | job | failure *kind* (not a diagnosis) |
|---|---|---|
| integument | barrier, heat, sensation | break in the barrier, heat loss, infection door |
| musculoskeletal | support, move, protect | loss of support or motion |
| nervous | fast signaling | loss of signal, too much signal, wrong target |
| endocrine | slower chemical signaling | too little / too much hormone as *kinds* |
| circulatory | move blood — gases, nutrients, waste, heat, signals | pump, volume, pipe, or blood-content failure |
| respiratory | gas exchange | airway, lung, drive, or blood-gas problem |
| digestive | break down, absorb, egest | intake, breakdown, absorb, or barrier problem |
| urinary | water, salt, nitrogen | filter, volume, or obstruction |
| immune / lymphatic | distinguish non-self / damaged-self and respond | under-response, over-response, mis-target |
| reproductive | gametes, pregnancy where that happens | fertility, pregnancy, infection as *kinds* |

**homeostasis** is keep-the-inside-in-range (ch 3). **failure** of a system is named as a syndrome or a disease on a clinical page — fetch; do not diagnose from this table.

worked literacy (one sentence each, then stop): “what does the liver *do*?” → name jobs (process nutrients, make proteins, clear some toxins) from an A&P door. “is my liver failing?” → clinician. the first is this chapter; the second is a person.

**imaging and labs** are measurements. a number without a reference range, a unit, and the lab’s method is not a result. ranges: MedlinePlus / the lab’s door. do not recite a “normal potassium” from memory.

**paired examples (literacy vs seat):**

| literacy (this book) | seat (not this book) |
|---|---|
| the heart is a pump; failure kinds are pump / volume / pipe | “is this chest pain a heart attack?” → EMS / clinician |
| insulin is a hormone that lets many cells take up glucose | insulin dose → clinician + label |
| vaccines train adaptive memory | schedule and “should I get X” → CDC page + clinician |
| kidneys filter and balance salt/water | creatinine cutoff as a diagnosis → clinician + the lab |

**check:** which system, which job. mechanism → biology. person → clinician.

## 3. range, vitals, failure

the inside stays in range: temperature, pH, osmolarity, glucose, gases, pressure — as *kinds* of variables. **values and cutoffs are a fetch.** do not recite a “normal” from memory.

**vital signs** (usual set): consciousness, breathing, pulse, blood pressure, temperature, sometimes oxygen saturation. they are observations. adult vs child vs infant ranges differ — fetch the table for the age. a vital is not a diagnosis; it is an input to a seat.

**shock** (circulatory): tissues not getting enough perfusion. kinds (names): hypovolemic, distributive, cardiogenic, obstructive. it is an emergency seat (ch 5–6), not a vibe.

**dehydration** is a water/salt deficit. **fever** is a regulated rise, not the same as hyperthermia. **inflammation** is a process (red, heat, swell, pain, loss of function as the classical names) — it is not automatically infection.

**pain** is a signal and a report. it does not by itself name the disease.

**check:** kind vs value. value → MedlinePlus / AHA / a pediatric table. person → clinician.

## 4. infection, vaccine, antibiotic

**infection** is a process: a pathogen in a host, replicating, doing harm. not a mood.

| kind | usual object |
|---|---|
| **bacteria** | prokaryote. cell wall stories. many antibiotics target here |
| **virus** | not a cell. hijacks. antibiotics do not treat a virus |
| **fungus** | eukaryote. different drugs |
| **parasite** | protozoa, helminth, ectoparasite as names |
| **prion** | misfolded protein as a named exception |

**transmission:** contact, droplet, airborne, vector, food/water, blood, vertical — name the route. **incubation** is a clock. a number of days for a named disease: fetch CDC / WHO.

**immune literacy.** innate vs adaptive. antibody, antigen, memory. **vaccine** trains the adaptive system with a specified antigen (or instruction to make one). it is not an antibiotic. **schedule** is a jurisdiction page (CDC / a named ministry) plus clinician. this book does not write a schedule.

**antibiotic.** a drug against bacteria (or a narrow other target as labeled). **stewardship:** the wrong drug, the wrong bug, the unfinished course as *ideas* — the label and the prescriber own the how. resistance is evolution (biology pack). do not name a molecule as a home kit.

**outbreak.** case definition, count, place, time: CDC / WHO / the named ministry. not a feed. not wiki.

**check:** virus vs bacterium. vaccine vs antibiotic. schedule → official page.

## 5. first aid as a system

first aid is **trained help until the seat arrives**. it is a sequence, not a personality.

jobs, in this order of *concern* (the living protocol’s exact order and counts change — fetch AHA / ILCOR / Red Cross before you teach a ratio):

1. **scene** — you do not become the second patient.
2. **help** — local emergency number; jurisdiction fetch.
3. **airway, breathing, circulation** — these are the jobs. CPR, choking, bleeding-control *steps and counts* are on the current certified page.
4. **do not move** a possible spine injury unless the scene forces it — the trained sequence says when.

**CPR** is a named protocol for unresponsive, not-breathing (or not-breathing-normally) people. compression, airway, defibrillator as *parts*. rate, depth, ratio, when to stop: **fetch**. a remembered “30:2” from a past course is not a SoT.

**AED** is a machine with prompts. turn it on; follow it. training beats this paragraph.

**choking, seizure, stroke, chest pain, anaphylaxis** are named emergencies. FAST / other screens are a fetch of the current public campaign, not a diagnosis. epinephrine auto-injector is a *prescribed* device; this book does not dose it.

**poison / ingestion.** do not induce vomiting from memory. poison-control door. container in hand if you have it.

**training.** a Red Cross / AHA / equivalent certificate is the lay seat. this chapter does not replace it.

**check:** scene. help. current protocol page. person in front of you → trained hands, not a textbook.

## 6. bleed, wound, burn, fracture, shock

structure of the injury. steps that are *not* a number:

**bleeding.** direct pressure on the wound is the usual first job. a tourniquet is a trained tool for life-threatening limb bleed. do not pack random powder. if it will not stop: emergency seat. **internal bleed** is a clinician/EMS job — you will not see the hole.

**wound.** break in the barrier. clean vs dirty is a judgment the protocol owns. tetanus is a vaccine story (ch 4) — schedule fetch. **do not** close a wound from this book.

**burn.** depth (superficial / partial / full as names) and *area* matter. cooling with water is a common first step on first-aid pages — duration and what not to put on the skin: fetch Red Cross / WHO. electrical and airway burns: emergency seat.

**fracture / sprain.** open vs closed. stabilize; do not reduce from this book. RICE/PRICE as mnemonics are dated and disputed — fetch the current page.

**shock** (ch 3). keep the person, get help, follow the trained sequence. do not give oral fluids to a person who may need a theater. kinds named; treatment not.

**head, eye, chest, abdomen, pregnancy, child** — special pages. fetch. this book does not improvise them.

**check:** trained sequence vs this paragraph. blood that will not stop → seat.

## 7. water, food, hygiene

**clean water** is a public-health system, not a vibe. **waterborne disease** is infection via water (ch 4). boil / filter / chemical — which method, which bug: fetch CDC / WHO.

**ORT (oral rehydration therapy)** is a named WHO/UNICEF treatment for dehydration from diarrhea: glucose + salts in water, in a ratio that is a **recipe on the WHO page**. do not invent grams. commercial ORS is a labeled product. a person who cannot drink, is unconscious, or is in shock: seat.

**hygiene.** hands, food, feces, cough, menstrual, wound. soap and water as the usual mechanical job. alcohol rub when the page says the hands are not visibly soiled — fetch CDC. **food safety:** cook, separate, chill as named jobs; temperatures are a fetch (USDA / FDA).

**nutrition** continues in ch 8. **air:** indoor smoke, CO, occupational dust — NIOSH / EPA / the named page.

**check:** water source. ORT recipe → WHO. temperature of a fridge → USDA, not memory.

## 8. nutrition

**nutrition** is what the body takes in and what it does with it. **macronutrients:** carbohydrate, protein, fat, plus water. **micronutrients:** vitamins and minerals as named classes.

**energy.** food energy is chemistry (biology pack). a kcal on a label is a measurement. a “requirement” is a DRI/RDA table — **NIH ODS / NAS** fetch. do not recite a daily calorie from memory.

**deficiency and excess** are both diseases. a named vitamin’s deficiency syndrome: fetch MedlinePlus. a supplement claim is often marketing; the label is FDA’s job; the dose is still a clinician/label job. **no dose from this book.**

**dietary pattern.** dietary guidelines are a jurisdiction document (US: dietaryguidelines.gov). they change. fetch.

**food allergy vs intolerance.** immune vs not. anaphylaxis is ch 5.

**check:** class of nutrient vs a gram amount. gram amount → label / DRI table.

## 9. drugs as a class

a **drug** is a substance used to change a process. **pharmacology** is what it does (mechanism, ADME). **pharmacy** is the licensed seat that dispenses.

| word | job |
|---|---|
| **indication** | what it is for, on the label |
| **contraindication** | when not |
| **mechanism** | biology + chemistry |
| **dose** | clinician + label. not this book |
| **adverse effect** | harm that can happen |
| **interaction** | two substances, one body |

**ADME** as names: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion. they explain why route and liver/kidney function matter. they do not set a milligram.

**routes:** oral, inhaled, IV, IM, topical, … name it. **OTC vs prescription** is a legal cut (FDA). **controlled substance** is a statute (`../law/`).

**generic vs brand** is a name cut. the molecule is PubChem / the label.

**do not** name a dose, an LD50, or a “take two.” **do not** stop a prescribed drug from this page.

**check:** mechanism vs indication vs dose. dose → stop.

## 10. public health and evidence

**public health** is health as a population: prevent, count, protect.

**surveillance.** case definition, denominator, place, time. a count without those is a story. CDC / WHO / a ministry.

**prevention.** primary (stop it starting), secondary (catch it early), tertiary (limit damage) as names.

**environmental.** water, air, vector, toxin, workplace (NIOSH / OSHA / EPA).

**equity.** who is exposed, who can reach a seat. a rate that is not stratified can lie.

**health system.** who pays, who delivers, who licenses — civics + law + this seat. this book does not pick a national system.

**how to read a health claim** (methods pack in this seat):

1. is it mechanism, a single study, a review, or a guideline?
2. who was studied (age, sex, comorbidity, setting)?
3. what was the comparison (placebo, standard care, nothing said)?
4. what is the **effect + uncertainty**, not the press-release relative risk alone?
5. does a society guideline already speak? fetch it. if the guideline and a new preprint disagree, say so; do not average.
6. if the next sentence would be a dose, a diagnosis, or “you should take X” → clinician. stop.

**check:** population vs this person. this person still goes to a clinician. the count goes to the agency page.

## 11. mind

**mental health** is function of mind and behavior as health. disorders are named in a clinical manual (DSM / ICD) — those texts are a fetch and a clinician’s tool, not a self-score from this chapter.

**literacy.** sleep, mood, anxiety, attention, substance, psychosis as *kinds of trouble*. a screen is not a diagnosis.

**crisis.** a person about to harm themselves or others is an emergency seat (local emergency number / a named crisis line — fetch the jurisdiction page). this book does not counsel a named person.

**substance.** intoxication, withdrawal, dependence as processes. harm reduction is a public-health page (CDC / SAMHSA / a ministry). no dose.

**check:** kind vs diagnosis. diagnosis → clinician. crisis → seat.

## 13. how to attack a problem

1. is this a **person** in front of you? if emergency — scene, help, trained sequence (ch 5). stop the textbook how.
2. is this a **dose / diagnosis / should I take X**? structure the ask, then clinician. do not name a dose.
3. name **structure vs process vs step**.
4. name the **system** (ch 2) and the **pathogen kind** if infectious (ch 4).
5. **mechanism** → biology pack. **number** → official page. **study design** → methods pack.
6. vaccine schedule / outbreak count / ORT recipe / CPR counts → LINK_INDEX, cite.
7. FOUND empty → **DONT_KNOW**.

| mechanism asked, dose given | biology + this fence |
