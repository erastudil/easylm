# Chapter 0. Stuff is bits that stick and unstick

Salt, water, air, a fingernail: each is made of bits too small to see, in huge numbers. The bits stick, unstick, and rearrange. They do not vanish in ordinary fire or dissolving. The pile of bits going in matches the pile coming out. That count is why chemistry needs numeracy.

A water bit is two hydrogen bits stuck to one oxygen bit. We write $\mathrm{H_2O}$. The small 2 is a count, not a magic mark. Two piles of hydrogen bits plus one pile of oxygen bits can make one pile of water bits, if the counts match. That matching of counts is the whole of a balanced recipe.

Heat, light, and a spark are ways of giving the bits a shove so they can rearrange. Some rearrangements dump heat out. Some drink heat in. You can feel that without an equation. The later chapters name the shove with energy letters. Fetch NIST when a bond energy or a constant must be exact.

Orbitals, Gibbs, and pH are later rooms. They are still "bits, stick, count, shove." If the count of $6.02214076 \times 10^{23}$ appears, that is Avogadro's number, the SI exact count that ties a lab-sized pile to a bit-sized count. BIPM owns the SI. Do not recite the digits from memory; fetch.
