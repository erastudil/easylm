# law — undergrad textbook

a working book for tongues, desks, and humans who must **name the system, the jurisdiction, and the operative text**.  
this file teaches. a holding, a section number, an element list for *this* court → DONT_KNOW / fetch the official door. **prep for a credentialed seat. this book does not file, appear, or sign. it is not legal advice.**

**law this book applies:** jurisdiction first. procedure is half. text before vibe (definitions, then shall/may, then except). holding ≠ dicta. burden has a standard. a right without a path is a poster. name the profession when a human is needed (attorney admitted *there*). white-hat fence stands — no evasion kit.

offices and elections: `../civics/`. this pack owns **systems and how to read**. US, Japan, Switzerland, and others appear as **named examples with official doors**, not as extra packs in this warehouse.

## 1. what law is

**law** is rules plus institutions plus **procedure that can coerce**. a custom without an office is a habit. a poster without a filing path is a wish.

four questions, every time:

1. **which system** (common law, civil law, mixed, religious, customary) and **which jurisdiction**?
2. **civil or criminal** (or administrative, or constitutional)?
3. what is the **operative text** (shall / may / must / except)?
4. who **decides** (court, agency, legislature, arbitrator)?

**rule of law** (working cluster, not a chant): rules are public, prospective, general, and applied by an office that can lose. philosophy pack for the argument; this pack for the office.

**not this book:** how to win a case, how to hide money, how to draft a filing for a living person. those are the admitted seat (ch 16).

**check:** if you cannot name the jurisdiction and the text, you are not ready to name a holding.

## 2. legal systems: common, civil, mixed

ideal types mix.

| type | usual mark |
|---|---|
| **common law** | cases are a source; precedent binds (stare decisis as the name); statutes sit on that practice |
| **civil law** | a **code** is the spine; cases illustrate; doctrine and commentary weigh heavy |
| **mixed** | both (Scotland, Louisiana, South Africa, Japan’s reception as named examples — fetch the map) |
| **religious** | a canon or sharia or halakha as a source for some subjects |
| **customary** | practice of a people, recognized or not by the state |

**common law** grew in English royal courts: the writ, the report, the later statute overlay, then export to colonies. **equity** was a second English court-system (conscience, injunction, trust) later fused in many places. do not recite a year; fetch if the fusion date is the job.

**civil law** grew from Roman texts through medieval universities into modern **codes** (French, German, Swiss as named families). the code is meant to be *the* statement. a case is an example of applying it. **doctrine** (treatises) can weigh as much as a case does in common law.

**US** is common law + a written constitution + federalism. doors: LII, congress.gov, uscode.house.gov, supremecourt.gov.  
**Japan** is civil law, codes, a postwar constitution. doors: e-gov, japaneselawtranslation.go.jp.  
**Switzerland** is civil law, codes + cantons + direct democracy. door: fedlex.admin.ch.

do not import a US element list into a civil-code problem. **consideration** is a common-law contract word. a Japanese or Swiss code has its own formation articles — fetch them.

this book is **not** an island-specific dump. a territory’s reception statute is a fetch on that gazette.

**check:** which system. which code or reporter.

## 3. sources of law

a **source** is what an office may cite as law.

| source | is |
|---|---|
| **constitution** | higher law. civics pack for the offices it creates |
| **statute / code** | text of the legislature (or a recodified whole) |
| **regulation / ordinance** | agency or city, *under* a statute |
| **case law** | what courts held (heavier in common law) |
| **treaty** | between states; domestic effect depends on the constitution |
| **custom** | practice plus opinio juris (international) or long use (some private law) |
| **general principles / doctrine** | named in some civil-code and international lists |

**hierarchy** (usual sketch, *varies*): constitution beats statute beats regulation. a case may interpret all three. if two same-level texts fight, the later or the more specific — **that conflict rule is itself a doctrine. fetch it.**

**primary vs secondary authority** (common-law library talk): the constitution, statute, regulation, and holding are primary. a restatement, encyclopedia, or law-review article is secondary — persuasive at best. Wikipedia is not authority.

**effective date.** a beautiful section that is not in force is a museum piece. cite title/section **and** the date you fetched.

**check:** which rung. in force? primary or commentary?

## 4. precedent

**precedent** is a past decision used as a reason in a later one.

**stare decisis** (“stand by things decided”) is the common-law name for *binding* precedent: a lower court in the same system follows a higher court’s holding on like facts. a court usually follows its own past, but can **overrule** (with a reason). **distinguishing** says the facts are not like enough.

**holding** is the rule the facts required. **dicta** is the rest of the talk. only the holding binds (usual school cut). a quoted sentence can be either.

**ratio decidendi / obiter dicta** are the English names for the same cut.

in **civil-law** systems, a single case usually does not bind as a statute binds. a *line* of cases, a high-court unification, or a published “jurisprudence constante” can still steer. fetch that system’s rule. do not shout “precedent” as if it were physics.

**persuasive** authority: another jurisdiction, a concurrence, a treatise. useful; not binding.

**check:** which court, which year, holding or dicta, binding or persuasive.

## 5. how to read a legal text

order of operations:

1. **caption and citation** (title, article, section, year).
2. **definitions** (often in front; they hijack ordinary English).
3. **operative clause** — shall / may / must / shall not.
4. **exceptions** and **provisos**.
5. **remedy / penalty / jurisdiction** at the back.
6. **cross-references**.

**shall** is duty (in the usual drafter’s dialect). **may** is permission. drafters sometimes fail that dialect — then the court has a job. do not invent a “plain meaning” that skips a definition.

**elements** are a list the claimant must meet. they live in a statute, a restatement, or a leading case — **fetch**. do not recite a four-part test from a mnemonic if the jurisdiction uses five.

**canons of construction** (school names): ordinary meaning, whole-text, no surplusage, specific over general, later over earlier, lenity in criminal cases (where it exists). they conflict. they are tools, not a vending machine. fetch the jurisdiction’s current habit.

**ambiguity vs vagueness.** ambiguity is two (or few) readings. vagueness is a fuzzy edge (how many trees make a forest). courts and agencies handle them differently. do not call every hard case “ambiguous.”

**legislative history** (committee reports, floor speech) is a *source some courts will look at* and some will not. it is not the statute. fetch the statute first.

**check:** definition → operative → exception. citation in hand.

## 5b. evidence (thin)

**evidence** is what a fact-finder may use. the **record** is what was actually put in.

school cuts (common-law names; codes differ):

| cut | usual job |
|---|---|
| **relevance** | does it move a fact of consequence |
| **authentication** | is this the thing it claims to be |
| **hearsay** | an out-of-court statement offered for its truth — many exceptions, all listed |
| **privilege** | some truths a court will not hear (lawyer–client as a named case) |
| **expert** | opinion from a field, under a reliability rule of *this* court |

TV “hearsay” is not a code. **fetch the evidence statute or rules of *this* court.** a civil-law dossier is a different machine: the judge often gathers; the parties still offer.

**check:** which fact. which rule. in or out of the record.

## 6. jurisdiction, venue, procedure

**jurisdiction** is power to speak. the wrong court is not a court. **subject-matter** (this kind of dispute) vs **personal** (this defendant) vs **territorial**. **venue** is which courthouse among those that have power.

**procedure is half the game.** notice, complaint or indictment, response, discovery or dossier, motion, hearing, judgment, **appeal clock**. miss a deadline and the right did not fire.

**civil procedure** (private dispute) and **criminal procedure** (the state accuses) are different machines. evidence rules sit on both. a “hearsay” instinct from TV is not a code.

**choice of law / private international law.** two jurisdictions, one fact pattern. a convention (Hague, CISG) or a code title — fetch. this book does not pick the law for a stranger’s contract.

**check:** can this office hear this. what is the clock.

## 7. due process

**due process** is the school name (US constitutional English) for a cluster: the state may not take life, liberty, or property without a **fair procedure**, and (in some doctrines) without a minimally fair *rule*. other systems say **fair hearing**, **natural justice**, **audi alteram partem**, **procès équitable**. name the local phrase; fetch the clause.

two working halves (US school cut — other systems slice differently):

| cut | usual ask |
|---|---|
| **procedural** | notice, chance to be heard, a neutral decision-maker, a record, a reason |
| **substantive** (contested) | whether the *rule itself* is within what the constitution will bear |

**notice** must actually inform a person who can understand it, in time to act. **hearing** can be paper, oral, or a full trial — the statute and the stakes decide. a parking ticket is not a criminal trial; a deportation is not a parking ticket.

**this is not a slogan.** miss the forum, the deadline, or the form, and the process did not fire.

**criminal due process cluster** (school names, common-law): accusation, counsel, public trial, confront witnesses, silence as a right against compelled self-incrimination, an independent fact-finder. **which of these exist here is a fetch.** other systems guarantee some of the same goods under other names, and not others.

**administrative due process** is often thinner: notice of the proposed action, a chance to submit, a reason. a license revocation is not a felony trial; it is also not a tweet.

**check:** which clause. which office. what notice, what hearing. fetch.

## 8. burden of proof

**burden** splits:

| name | is |
|---|---|
| **burden of pleading** | who must allege the element |
| **burden of production** | who must put on enough evidence to let a fact-finder consider it |
| **burden of persuasion** | who loses if the fact-finder cannot decide |

**standard** is how sure the fact-finder must be (common-law school names):

| name | usual home |
|---|---|
| **preponderance of the evidence** | most civil cases: more likely than not |
| **clear and convincing** | some civil (fraud, some family, some constitutional) |
| **beyond a reasonable doubt** | criminal conviction (usual) |

these are **names**. the instruction in *this* court is a fetch. civil-law systems may speak of inner conviction, free evaluation of evidence, or a code article — fetch it. do not export a US jury instruction into a code court.

**presumptions** shift a burden until rebutted. **affirmative defenses** (self-defense, statute of limitations as names) often put a burden on the defendant — fetch who carries what.

**check:** who must prove. which standard. which instruction.

## 9. civil and criminal

**criminal law:** the sovereign accuses; liberty or a fine or both; the state bears a high burden (usual). **civil law** (the *dispute* sense, not the *system*): private parties, damages or injunction, lower burden (usual). one event can be both (a battery and a prosecution).

**public law** (constitutional, administrative, criminal) vs **private law** (contract, tort, property, family) is a civil-law map that still helps. US courses often cut “bar subjects” instead.

**remedy.** damages, specific performance, injunction, restitution, declaration, imprisonment, probation — name which. a right without a remedy is a poster (civics said the same of procedure).

**check:** who is the plaintiff. what they want. which burden.

## 10. contract

a **contract** is a bargain the law will notice. **common-law school list:** offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality. **civil-code formation** is offer/acceptance plus cause or other articles — **fetch the code**. do not ship consideration into a civil-code deal.

**writing.** some deals need a writing (statute of frauds as a common-law name; notarial form in some civil systems). fetch the formality.

**breach and excuse.** performance, material breach, frustration, impossibility, force majeure as *names*. the clause in *this* contract is the first text; the code is the background.

**third parties, assignment, CISG** (international sale of goods): UNCITRAL / the convention text if the deal is in that world.

**check:** which system’s formation. which writing. which governing-law clause.

---

## 11. tort and delict

**tort** (common law) / **delict** (civil law) is civil wrong outside (or beside) contract.

**common-law school list:** duty, breach, causation, damage. **intentional / negligence / strict** as families. **civil-code** often has a general clause (fault, damage, causal link) plus special regimes. fetch the article.

**cause.** but-for and proximate as common-law names; adequate cause as a cousin. methods pack: association is cheap; law still needs its own test — the *instruction* is a fetch.

**damages.** compensatory vs punitive (punitive is not universal). do not invent a multiplier.

**check:** duty from where (statute, common law, code article). harm named.

## 12. property

**property** is a bundle of claims about a thing: use, exclude, transfer, residual. **real** (land) vs **personal** / movables. **title** vs **possession**. a registry (land book, Torrens, recording act) is a jurisdiction machine — fetch.

**estates and future interests** are common-law land tech. **usufruct / superficies** are civil-code cousins. do not mix the vocabularies in one sentence without saying so.

**intellectual** property is ch 13 (a different office).

**takings / expropriation** is public law plus a compensation clause — constitution of that state.

**check:** what the thing is. which registry. which jurisdiction’s land code.

## 13. intellectual property

four kinds:

| kind | usual office (examples) |
|---|---|
| **patent** | invention, claims, term statutory — USPTO / JPO / EPO / the named office |
| **copyright** | original work of authorship — copyright.gov / the named ministry |
| **trademark** | mark in trade, classes — USPTO / the named registry |
| **trade secret** | secrecy plus a statute |

term, territoriality, and what counts as protectable: **fetch**. a Berne / Paris / TRIPS sentence is a treaty sentence — WIPO / WTO texts. **open source** is a copyright license; read it.

**check:** which kind. which territory. which office.

## 14. administrative law

the **agency** writes rules, finds facts, grants licenses, spends. the **leash** is a statute (civics). **notice-and-comment**, hearing, record, appeal to a court — names of a path. US APA: fetch Cornell LII / uscode.house.gov. other states: the admin-procedure act of that state — fetch.

**deference.** how much a court bows to an agency’s reading is a doctrine with a year. do not recite a US case name as if it were a civil-code article.

**check:** organic statute. which process. which reviewing court.

## 15. public international law (thin)

**public international law** is law *between* states (and some other subjects): treaties, custom, general principles, and the ICJ’s statute list. **it is not a world legislature.**

**treaty.** pacta sunt servanda as the named starting point. text: UN treaty collection. **domestic effect** depends on the constitution (civics).

**ICJ, ICC, WTO DSB, human-rights treaty bodies** are different offices with different parties. fetch the statute of *that* court. this book does not hail anyone before The Hague.

**check:** between states or inside one. which tribunal’s statute.

## 16. the professional seat — not legal advice

this textbook **does not** take a client, file a paper, or sign a deed. **it is not legal advice.**

when the ask is a filing, a defense, a will, a closing, a charging document: **name the seat** — attorney / solicitor / barrister / *bengoshi* / *avocat* **admitted in that jurisdiction**. a notary is a different seat in civil-law countries. an agent before a patent office is a different roster.

here is the kind of human; these are the credentials. a local directory when they give a place. do not impersonate.

**ethics.** confidentiality, conflicts, candor to the tribunal — the named bar’s rules. fetch.

**check:** which jurisdiction’s roll. which seat.

## 18. how to attack a problem

1. **jurisdiction** and **system**.
2. **civil / criminal / admin / constitutional**.
3. find the **operative text**. read definitions first.
4. **procedure** — clock, notice, the right office. due process is ch 7.
5. **burden and standard** — ch 8.
6. if a **filing or a defense** — ch 16. stop the how-to-sue from this book.
7. elements of a claim → fetch that jurisdiction’s list.
8. FOUND empty → **DONT_KNOW**.
